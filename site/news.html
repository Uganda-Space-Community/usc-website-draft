Uganda Signs Artemis Accords

15 Jul 2026 · Policy & Governance

Uganda Commits to Responsible Space Exploration

Uganda has officially signed the Artemis Accords, becoming the latest African nation to commit to the principles of responsible, peaceful, and sustainable exploration and use of outer space. The signing ceremony took place at the Ministry of Science and Technology headquarters in Kampala.

The Artemis Accords, led by the United States in partnership with NASA, establish a set of norms for civilian space exploration and the use of the Moon, Mars, comets, and asteroids for peaceful purposes. By signing, Uganda joins a growing coalition of nations committed to transparency, interoperability, and the release of scientific data.

What This Means for Uganda

This milestone opens the door for Ugandan researchers and institutions to participate in international space missions and data-sharing agreements. It also strengthens Uganda's position in regional and continental space governance discussions, particularly within the African Space Agency framework.

The Uganda Space Agency will serve as the primary coordinating body for implementing the accords' principles, working closely with universities, research institutions, and international partners.

Key Provisions

The accords commit signatories to peaceful exploration, transparency in space operations, the release of scientific data, protection of heritage sites, deconfliction of activities, and the responsible management of space resources. Uganda's signing aligns with its broader national space policy goals.
